# Background Check Relationship Disclosure Boundary

Reviewed: 2026-06-24

This is public source-quality guidance, not legal advice.

## Core Rule

Background-check language without relationship disclosure is confidence selling.

A background check is a scoped screening event, not a universal trust badge. Compliance language without compliance controls is confidence selling. Provider screening is not transaction compliance.

## The Pattern

A customer may vet a company, service brand, directory, training community, or platform-facing provider because public pages say background-checked, certified, vetted, compliant, secure, white-glove, expert, or managed.

The work may then be dispatched to a non-employee notary, independent contractor, marketplace responder, social-group respondent, directory lead, or signing-service network member. If the customer is not told that relationship, the trust language can make the brand look more controlled than the actual fulfillment model.

This is not only a Facebook problem. The same boundary can appear in signing services, Snapdocs-style marketplaces, notary directories, platform assignment networks, private groups, social posts, public search snippets, and broker-like notary referral systems.

## What A Background Check Can Show

- A provider, association, platform, directory, or hiring party says some screening exists.
- A private work-access rule or marketplace rule may affect who receives work.
- A profile or credential packet may include commission, screening, certification, E&O, training, or directory fields.
- A background-check product or status may be part of a private credential rail.

## What It Does Not Prove

- the person performing the notarial act is an employee
- the customer was told the worker relationship
- the company controls transaction-level compliance
- the screening was current for the transaction date
- the screening covered every relevant jurisdiction or record source
- the screened person has no record
- the screened person is transaction-competent
- the notary state, platform workflow, identity method, certificate, output lane, retained record, recipient path, or apostille/authentication route fits the transaction

## Questions To Ask

- Who is the signer-facing provider or service brand?
- Who is the commissioned notary actually performing the act?
- Is the notary an employee, independent contractor, marketplace responder, signing-service assignee, directory lead, or social-group referral?
- Was that relationship disclosed to the customer or signer before purchase or session?
- Who requested the background check, and for what role, date, scope, jurisdiction, and standard?
- Was any consumer-report-derived field used for onboarding, ranking, assignment, visibility, suspension, or rejection?
- Who selected the notary state and platform, and did the platform fit that state law and transaction date?
- Who verified transaction-level compliance?

## Safe Public Wording

The public claim shows a screening or credential claim, not by itself controlled fulfillment or transaction-level compliance.

The customer-facing issue is relationship and control disclosure: who is actually doing the notarial act, under what relationship, and who owns transaction compliance.

Background-check and certification language can be work-access evidence. It should not be used as a universal trust badge or as proof that a complex RON route is supportable.

## Public Boundary

Do not publish customer facts, private phone-call details, private correspondence, signer documents, payment data, IDs, private endpoint details, or unsupported personal accusations.

Use public marketing claims, public directory or marketplace claims, public-safe social-sourcing patterns, official FTC/CFPB/EEOC/state sources, and existing public-safe answer-engine correction records.
