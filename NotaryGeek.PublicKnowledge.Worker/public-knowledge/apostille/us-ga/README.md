# Georgia apostille and Great Seal: choose the authority first

**Reviewed:** September 28, 2026 (U.S. Eastern). This guide concerns the **U.S. state of Georgia (GA)**, not the country of Georgia (GE). [Structured route and claim sources](us-ga-apostille-authentication.json). It is public research, not a live service, deadline, or recipient-acceptance promise.

## One state, two foreign-use authorities

The [Georgia Superior Court Clerks’ Cooperative Authority (GSCCCA)](https://www.gsccca.org/notary-and-apostilles/apostilles/general-apostille-information) issues Georgia apostilles for use in the Apostille Convention path. For a destination **outside** that path, the [Georgia Secretary of State](https://sos.ga.gov/page/great-seal-authentication-administrative-services) handles Great Seal authentication. Determine the actual destination and its Convention relationship with the United States before choosing an office. A foreign embassy or consulate located in the U.S. is treated by GSCCCA as international use for the foreign country, not automatically domestic use.

| Route | Authority | Key distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Hague Apostille Convention path | [GSCCCA apostille office](https://www.gsccca.org/notary-and-apostilles/apostilles/general-apostille-information) | GSCCCA says **no additional county certification** is needed before its apostille for a Georgia-notarized document. |
| Outside Convention path | [Secretary of State Great Seal authentication](https://sos.ga.gov/page/great-seal-authentication-administrative-services) | For notarized documents, its guidance requires a notary certification from the Clerk of Superior Court **in the notary’s commissioning county** before Great Seal authentication. Official public records have their own source/signature requirements. |
| U.S. domestic certification of a notary | GSCCCA or the commissioning county Clerk of Superior Court | GSCCCA says its certification of notary is for domestic/U.S. territory/U.S. embassy uses and **is not combined with an apostille**. |

This is why “get the county clerk first” is a route error when applied to every Georgia apostille. It belongs to a different branch. Keep the Notary Geek **Public-Official Signature Route** visible: identify the signature being authenticated and the office authorized for that destination.

## Match the document to the correct form

- **Vital records and court records:** GSCCCA asks for an official certified copy with the issuing Georgia official’s signature and seal. Publicly recorded documents are not converted into official certified copies by notarizing an ordinary photocopy. For older vital records, GSCCCA recommends a newer certified copy if the issuing signature may no longer be on file. [Official details](https://www.gsccca.org/notary-and-apostilles/apostilles/general-apostille-information).
- **Corporations Division records:** For the apostille branch, GSCCCA accepts a certified copy issued by the current Georgia Secretary of State, including an official certified download. This is distinct from a private company resolution or affidavit notarized by a Georgia notary. The Secretary of State’s **Great Seal** page describes a different condition for printed corporate records, so do not carry the apostille output rule across branches. [GSCCCA](https://www.gsccca.org/notary-and-apostilles/apostilles/general-apostille-information) · [Great Seal](https://sos.ga.gov/page/great-seal-authentication-administrative-services).
- **Georgia-notarized private documents:** GSCCCA’s apostille guidance calls for the notarial certificate, exact date, hand-signed ink signature matching the commission, and a clear matching seal. The outside-Convention Great Seal route adds the commissioning-county clerk certification for a notarized document. [GSCCCA](https://www.gsccca.org/notary-and-apostilles/apostilles/general-apostille-information) · [Great Seal](https://sos.ga.gov/page/great-seal-authentication-administrative-services).
- **Printed digital certified court records:** GSCCCA expressly says printed digital certified divorce decrees from participating county eCertification programs can receive an apostille. That narrow public-record allowance does **not** establish acceptance of every printed electronic notarization. [GSCCCA](https://www.gsccca.org/notary-and-apostilles/apostilles/general-apostille-information).
- **Federal, out-of-state, or foreign-issued records:** GSCCCA says these do not belong in its Georgia apostille queue. Route by the authenticating public official and the relevant authority. [GSCCCA exclusions](https://www.gsccca.org/notary-and-apostilles/apostilles/general-apostille-information).

## Submission and the current walk-in question

GSCCCA lists **$3 per apostille document** and describes normal mail-in processing as **one to two business days**. Its mail instructions call for destination country and contact details, payment, and a prepaid return envelope or computer-generated FedEx/UPS airbill. The [request form](https://www.gsccca.org/docs/notary-documents/apostillerequestform.pdf) is a helpful official example; the guidance also permits a cover letter or instructional note. The Secretary of State lists **$10 per Great Seal document** and a **three to five business day current timeline**. These are agency statements to recheck before submission, not shipment or recipient deadlines. [GSCCCA](https://www.gsccca.org/notary-and-apostilles/apostilles/general-apostille-information) · [Great Seal](https://sos.ga.gov/page/great-seal-authentication-administrative-services).

**Walk-in status is unresolved from the published pages.** GSCCCA’s general page advertises walk-in hours, while its [temporary closure notice](https://www.gsccca.org/learn/walk-in-service-suspended) still says walk-in service was suspended from April 16, 2026 and expected to resume July 1, 2026. Both remained accessible when checked September 28. Get current confirmation before relying on hand delivery; mail and the drop-box route are described in the notice. This is a dated **Source-Quality Failure** to track, not a reason to choose whichever page sounds more convenient.

## Evidence still needed

Check the [HCCH status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41) for the actual destination and applicability to the United States. Record the recipient’s written document, translation, age, and output requirements as **Recipient Evidence**. Confirm a particular electronic or remote notarial output with the competent authority: the reviewed GSCCCA general page describes ink-signed notarizations and separately allows certain printed digital **certified court records**. Those are different document lanes.
