# Kansas apostille and authentication: agent route guide

**Reviewed:** September 28, 2026 (U.S. Eastern). **Coverage:** Kansas public-official and Kansas-notary signatures. This is a public research guide, not a live service commitment or a guarantee that a recipient will accept a document. [Machine-readable record](us-ks-apostille-authentication.json).

## Start with the signature

Ask whose public-official signature or certification must be authenticated. The [Kansas Secretary of State](https://sos.ks.gov/general-services/apostilles-authentications.html) says it issues apostilles and authentications for Kansas documents and verifies the seal and signature of a Kansas public official or Kansas notary. A document about a Kansas company can follow a different state’s notary route if another state’s notarial signature is the one to authenticate. That latter example applies Notary Geek’s [Public-Official Signature Route](https://notary.cx/notarial-routing-model.json); it is an inference from the model, not a quote from the Kansas agency.

Then identify the destination country. Kansas describes an **apostille** for a destination in the 1961 Apostille Convention path and an **authentication** for a country outside that path. [Form DC](https://www.sos.ks.gov/forms/administration/DC.pdf) asks for the country. Check current treaty status and the actual recipient’s written requirements for the specific transaction. For a Convention destination, Kansas says its state apostille does not need another U.S. Department of State certification or embassy/consular legalization. Outside that path, research the separate downstream legalization requirements rather than calling the route “apostille plus extra steps.” [Kansas source](https://sos.ks.gov/general-services/apostilles-authentications.html).

## Match the document to its source

| Document in hand | What to check first | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| Kansas birth, marriage, death, or divorce record | Obtain the original or certified copy from the issuing authority; Kansas points to the Office of Vital Statistics for certified vital records. | [Apostille instructions](https://sos.ks.gov/general-services/apostilles-authentications.html) |
| Privately created document notarized by a Kansas notary | Identify the Kansas notary signature, notarial certificate, and whether the actual document/output fits the submission requirement. | [Apostille instructions](https://sos.ks.gov/general-services/apostilles-authentications.html) |
| Kansas company filing | A free file-stamped copy is distinct from an official certified copy. Kansas sells certified copies and certificates of good standing online; determine which record the recipient requires. | [Copies and Certifications](https://sos.ks.gov/businesses/copies-and-certifications.html) |
| Kansas court judgment | Kansas says the court official’s signature must be on file; Form DC asks for the official’s printed name. | [Apostille instructions](https://sos.ks.gov/general-services/apostilles-authentications.html) and [Form DC](https://www.sos.ks.gov/forms/administration/DC.pdf) |
| Private document about a Kansas LLC notarized in another state | The company’s Kansas connection does not answer which public-official signature is being authenticated. Research the notary state’s route. | [Notary Geek route model](https://notary.cx/notarial-routing-model.json) |

Kansas lists other public-authority or Kansas-notarized documents, including foreign-language documents when the notarization is in English. That category is not blanket approval for every format or destination. [Kansas source](https://sos.ks.gov/general-services/apostilles-authentications.html).

## What Kansas asks you to submit

The office asks for completed [Form DC](https://www.sos.ks.gov/forms/administration/DC.pdf), the **original document or a certified copy**, and its fee. The state page lists **$10 per document**, effective March 2, 2026. Form DC, revised June 17, 2026, says requests are **generally processed within 3–5 business days after the office receives them**. Those days are an agency estimate, not a delivery, recipient-acceptance, or service deadline. Check both official sources again before payment or shipment. [Agency page](https://sos.ks.gov/general-services/apostilles-authentications.html) · [Form DC](https://www.sos.ks.gov/forms/administration/DC.pdf).

Send or deliver the packet to the address printed on the current form and agency page. For return, Form DC offers a prepaid self-addressed envelope or a supplied shipping label that can be domestic or international. Its separate, office-arranged **FedEx Option 2 requires a U.S. physical address** and explicitly disallows international shipping under that option. Do not collapse those two choices into “Kansas will FedEx internationally.” [Form DC](https://www.sos.ks.gov/forms/administration/DC.pdf).

## Questions this record does not yet settle

- Kansas permits RON in its [notary guidance](https://sos.ks.gov/general-services/notary.html), but the reviewed apostille page does not specify whether a particular electronic output, printed electronic notarization, or e-apostille route will be accepted. Establish that for the actual output before promising a route.
- An ordinary scan of a paper notarization is not the original or an official certified copy described in the submission instructions. Keep the **Paper Original Lane** separate from any **Remote/Electronic Output Lane** until the competent authority confirms the latter.
- Certification of a public-official signature does not establish that the receiving institution accepts the document’s contents, translation, issue date, or form. Record **Recipient Evidence** separately.
- Federal documents, foreign-origin records, and signatures from other states require their own authority research; this Kansas record does not route them.
