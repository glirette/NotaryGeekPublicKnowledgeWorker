# California apostille and authentication: agent route guide

**Single-status/no-record evidence reviewed October 31, 2026; earlier routes retain their claim dates · Partial California route pilot.** [Structured record](us-ca-apostille-authentication.json) · [October 31 evidence](source-notes-2026-10-31.md) · [16 single-status cases](adversarial-cases-2026-10-31.json) · [October 28 evidence](source-notes-2026-10-28.md) · [12 marriage cases](adversarial-cases-2026-10-28.json) · [October 22 evidence](source-notes-2026-10-22.md) · [17 earlier cases](adversarial-cases-2026-10-22.json) · [coverage map](../coverage.json)

Start with the **Public-Official Signature Route**: identify the issuer, the signature to be authenticated, the actual output, and the recipient's requested document. California SOS authenticates California public-official signatures for foreign use. An apostille concerns signature, official capacity and seal, not the truth of the document's contents. The recipient's acceptance remains a separate question. [SOS request](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/request-apostille) · [FAQ 1](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/apostille-faqs)

## Single status: no-record result or private assertion

**Checked October 31, 2026.** First establish the recipient's requested evidence. A county search result, CDPH Certificate of No Public Record (CNPR), private eligibility affidavit and prior divorce judgment answer different questions. The [State Department](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/living-abroad/marriage.html) describes foreign requirements and possible personal eligibility statements; it does not establish one universal document. In an apostille, **official capacity** means the signing official's role, not the holder's legal capacity to marry. [SOS FAQ 1](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/apostille-faqs)

| Presented or requested evidence | Decision and signature route |
| --- | --- |
| Recipient requests a San Diego no-record search letter | Use the county's published procurement procedure below. JSON `single-status-san-diego-order` offers **only the county order lane**, not an SOS submission lane. |
| Issued San Diego Letter of No Record | Preserve the search limits and actual certification. Identify its official signer and paper form; resolve **CA-Q5** before claiming SOS readiness. JSON `single-status-san-diego-letter`. |
| CDPH CNPR | It is a certified unsuccessful application-based search, not an established comprehensive single-status service. Exact CNPR signer/output and any intermediate step remain **CA-Q6**. JSON `single-status-cdph-cnpr`. |
| Recipient accepts a private single-status affidavit | The declarant supplies the statement; a lawful California notarization supplies the California notary signature for the ordinary SOS route. Preserve the complete original package. It remains a private assertion, not a government record search. JSON `single-status-private-affidavit`. Another jurisdiction's notary must be routed by that signature, not the declarant's California residence. |
| Recipient requires issuer search evidence; only a private affidavit is available | Hold the substitution. Obtain the requested record or explicit acceptance of the alternative; no SOS lane is offered. JSON `single-status-recipient-mismatch`. |
| Application, scan, verification screen or customer printout | Obtain the actual issued result and exact presentation instructions. No accepted electronic-to-paper route follows from online access or a verification code. JSON `single-status-output-copy`; **CA-Q5/CA-Q6**. |
| Letter confirming a confidential marriage exists | This is positive confirmation, neither a negative search nor the restricted certified marriage record. Apply the existing confidential-record branches if that record is required. JSON `single-status-confidential-confirmation`. |
| Prior divorce judgment | For a California-issued judgment, use `court-divorce-judgment` for required termination evidence and its **CA-Q2** signer check. Other jurisdictions' court records follow their actual issuing authority. One terminated marriage does not establish absence of later marriage or every present eligibility condition. JSON `single-status-prior-divorce`. |

**San Diego procurement:** its [LNR page](https://www.sdarcc.gov/content/arcc/home/divisions/recorder-clerk/birth-death-marriage-certificate/single-status-affidavit.html) directs applicants to the [VIT720 marriage application](https://www.sdarcc.gov/content/dam/arcc/recorder-county-clerk/forms/vitals/Marriage%20Certificate%20Application%20In%20Person%20and%20Mail_20251231.pdf) (Rev. December 31, 2025). Mark it LNR and supply full name and date of birth as the webpage directs. **No sworn statement or notarization is required for LNR.** Include the form's return/contact details and **$19 per letter** by check or money order, payable per the webpage to San Diego Recorder/County Clerk. Mail to **ATTN: Vital Records, P.O. Box 121750, San Diego, CA 92112-1750**. The form uses the shorter payee wording San Diego County Recorder. Another person may request in person for someone abroad; the form asks in-person requesters for photo ID. The service is unavailable at Kearny Mesa. No appointment is required at published participating offices; recheck hours. LNR-specific tracking, overseas return and delivery guarantees are not established. This fee and return stage are separate from SOS.

**Read negative evidence narrowly.** San Diego's [record page](https://www.sdarcc.gov/content/arcc/home/divisions/recorder-clerk/birth-death-marriage-certificate/marriage-certificate.html) limits custody to licenses issued there. The LNR form ties a failed search to supplied information. The procedure does not establish all searched years, aliases or confidential indexes. Do not assume confidential records are included **or excluded**. The LNR page's broad single-status wording does not make a county result proof of no marriage anywhere.

[CDPH FAQ 6–7 and 22](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CHSI/Pages/Vital-Records-Frequently-Asked-Questions-%28FAQs%29.aspx) describes CNPR as no record located using the application's information, with the search fee retained. Its [availability warning](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CHSI/Pages/Vital-Records-Important-Information.aspx) says a record not yet in the database can produce CNPR, and directs early marriage requests to the county. CDPH lacks confidential marriage records and has year gaps; the October 28 availability discrepancy remains visible below. These CDPH findings use official indexed text because direct access returned 502. They do not establish a person-wide search service, exact issued certificate or completed order.

**Authentication remains a separate gate.** Neither VIT720 nor the CDPH descriptions show the actual issued no-record certifier/signature/seal. Obtain that information and the accepted paper form before selecting SOS mail or counter submission. Do not apply the SOS birth/death exception or San Diego's [other signature-authentication services](https://www.sdarcc.gov/content/arcc/home/divisions/recorder-clerk/signature-authentication.html) as an automatic LNR chain. A new private notarization changes the signature route; it does not turn a copy into an issuer letter. The [California jurat disclaimer](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/jurats) separates signer identity from the document's truth, accuracy and validity. Even an authenticated document still needs separate recipient acceptance of its scope, age, translation and delivery form.

## Marriage records: access, copy type and certifying signature

Start with the **registered marriage record** and the county that issued the license. A ceremony in a different county does not move the record there. Public records come from the license-issuing **County Recorder**; confidential records come from that **County Clerk**. CDPH provides public records for specified years and does not hold confidential marriages. [CDPH directory](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CHSI/pages/county-registrars-and-recorders.aspx) (official indexed text) · [San Diego record instructions](https://www.sdarcc.gov/content/arcc/home/divisions/recorder-clerk/birth-death-marriage-certificate/marriage-certificate.html)

| What is requested or presented? | Decision and next step |
| --- | --- |
| Authorized copy of a public marriage record | Establish eligibility with the custodian, then obtain the issuer-certified paper record. The apostille targets the **official certifying that copy**, not the wedding officiant or the notary on the copy application. JSON: `marriage-public-authorized`. |
| Confidential marriage certificate | Use the license-issuing County Clerk. Access is limited to a party to the marriage or court-order entitlement; public-record family relationships do not automatically qualify. No confidential informational copy is available. JSON: `marriage-confidential-certified`. |
| Courier or relative without confidential-record entitlement | Stop procurement until access is established. SOS permission to submit someone else's document does not grant permission to obtain their confidential record. JSON: `marriage-confidential-access`; no submission lane is offered at this stage. |
| Certified informational public-marriage copy | It **is certified**, but cannot establish identity. Exact SOS presentation and recipient acceptance remain separate questions, **CA-Q4**. An apostille does not remove the informational restriction. Do not assert categorical apostille acceptance or rejection. JSON: `marriage-informational`. |
| Intact San Mateo Clerk-Recorder authorized paper marriage certificate | San Mateo explicitly permits its Clerk-Recorder-issued marriage certificate to go directly to SOS without additional documentation. Apply the ordinary SOS request package below. Do not generalize this to unusual signers, informational copies, electronic outputs or all other counties. JSON: `marriage-san-mateo-paper`. |
| License awaiting registration, application, or decorative keepsake | Obtain the registered certified record. San Diego's county-issued gold-seal keepsake is expressly a souvenir, even when its confidential mail application was notarized. JSON: `marriage-application-or-keepsake`. This does not decide the legal validity of a marriage whose record has not yet been registered. |
| Scan, private photocopy or customer printout | Follow the issuer paper route or obtain exact presentation authority. Online ordering is not evidence of accepted electronic output; a new private statement is not the custodian's certified record. JSON: `marriage-online-or-private-copy`. |

[Sacramento eligibility and copy types](https://ccr.saccounty.gov/us/en/marriage-certificates.html) · [SOS FAQ 5, third-party submission](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/apostille-faqs) · [San Mateo direct route](https://smcacre.gov/county-clerk-recorder/apostille-information) · [San Diego keepsake](https://www.sdarcc.gov/content/arcc/home/divisions/recorder-clerk/marriage-license-civil-ceremony/marriage-keepsake.html). Sacramento cites HSC 103526 and Family Code 509/511; current legislative pages were inaccessible during this review. These are narrow agency-procedure findings, not a complete statutory analysis.

**Two different signatures:** the notarized applicant statement obtains an authorized copy; the issuing official certifies the resulting record. San Diego allows an out-of-California notary venue on its application. That does not change the returned California record's authority to the application notary's state. SOS FAQ 3's county-certification example expressly concerns **birth/death**, so it supplies no automatic extra marriage step.

**A concrete paper path:** use San Mateo's [current linked application](https://smcacre.gov/media/1134/download?attachment=) and applicable notarized statement, identifying the marriage and license county. Its [ordering page](https://smcacre.gov/county-clerk-recorder/marriage-certificates) directs mail to **San Mateo County Clerk-Recorder, Attn: Vital Records, 555 County Center, 1st Floor, Redwood City, CA 94063**, with a return address and daytime phone. The linked form and [fee table](https://smcacre.gov/county-clerk-recorder/vital-records-fees) list **$19** for a general-public copy. Payment is by preprinted U.S.-bank check or money order payable to the Clerk-Recorder; return is regular mail or a supplied prepaid overnight envelope. Obtain the issued record, preserve its certification, then use the separate SOS submission and return package. No combined fee or deadline is implied.

**Issuer-specific application rules matter overseas.** [CDPH FAQ 24](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CHSI/Pages/Vital-Records-Frequently-Asked-Questions-%28FAQs%29.aspx) lists a foreign notary with an apostille as one option; San Mateo's ordering page excludes foreign-notary acknowledgments and specifies listed U.S. consular officials or a qualifying foreign court judge. These govern different offices' applications. Confirm the chosen custodian's rule before executing the application; neither rule determines the signature on the later marriage certificate.

**CDPH alternative and limits:** FAQ 25 says to state “apostille” as the purpose of a certified-copy request and that further SOS instructions will follow. This is not a CDPH-issued apostille or combined service. The [dedicated marriage page](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CHSI/Pages/Vital-Records-Obtaining-Certified-Copies-of-Marriage-Records.aspx) lists **1905–1999, 2001 and 2008–2025**; FAQ 8 instead lists **1905–1999 and 2008–2023**. Both were available only through official indexed text after direct retrieval failed. Confirm the year or use the license-issuing county; an absent year does not prove that no marriage record exists. CDPH's FAQ also says it does not supply digital/scanned/faxed vital-record images.

The [October 28 notes](source-notes-2026-10-28.md) preserve these source scopes and unresolved **CA-Q4**: exact marriage certifier, informational-copy and electronic presentation. A supported issuer procedure is not approval of an unseen certificate, nor evidence of a foreign recipient's required copy type, translation or delivery condition.

## Company records: certification before presentation

| What is in hand? | Decision and next step |
| --- | --- |
| Free business-search image or plain copy | Obtain an SOS-certified filing copy if an official certified record is required. A printed filing image, company status search, or private custodian statement is not that certification. |
| Online-certified filing copy or certificate of status | Preserve the complete official output. **Paper presentation remains unresolved:** the reviewed public instructions do not say whether the customer's printout is accepted for apostille, or requires agency-issued paper certification. Verification alone does not answer that question. This is not a finding that all such printouts are rejected. See **CA-Q1** below. |
| Agency-issued paper certified filing or status certificate | Inspect the certification, actual official signature and complete pages, then follow SOS apostille instructions for that returned record. The published paper procurement route is a supported alternative to assuming online-to-paper acceptance; it is not proof of acceptance of an unseen record. |
| Private bylaws or operating agreement | These are maintained by the entity, not filed with SOS. If privately notarized, follow the notary's signature. An SOS status certificate authenticates neither these papers nor their substantive contents. |
| Another state's formation record | Follow that issuer's authority. A California registration/status certificate about a foreign-qualified company is a different California record; it does not authenticate the other state's formation certificate. |

The [records page](https://www.sos.ca.gov/business-programs/business-entities/information-requests) distinguishes certified records from plain copies and private company documents. The [bizfile hub](https://www.sos.ca.gov/business-programs/bizfile) separately lists copy verification and apostille services. Neither supplies a public instruction authorizing the precise printout conversion above. These distinctions are encoded in JSON branch `sos-business-certified`, facts `company-online-presentation-unresolved` and `company-supported-paper-alternative`, and cases CA-20261022-01 through -05.

The **March 2026 [Business Entities Records Order Form](https://bpd.cdn.sos.ca.gov/pdf/be-records-requests.pdf)** includes corporations, LLCs and LPs as well as GP/LLP and other listed types. Select the needed certified filing or status certificate, not a plain copy. It lists $5 per certified document or status certificate. Paper orders go to **BE Certification and Records, P.O. Box 944260, Sacramento, CA 94244-2600**, or drop off at **1500 11th Street, 3rd floor, Room 380**. Mail orders return by mail; drop-off allows mail or pickup and adds $10 per entity. The check/money-order address controls correspondence where applicable. Record procurement and apostille submission are separate stages, with separate fees and addresses; the form does not establish a combined service.

For entity types other than Corp/LLC/LP, the records page specifies a manually stamped certification on the final page's reverse, identifying date, Secretary signature, seal, page count and deputy initials. Do not assume that exact layout applies to every Corp/LLC/LP paper output. California's rules for electronic signatures on filings **submitted to** SOS also do not decide treatment of certified outputs **issued by** SOS.

## Court-issued divorce and judgment records

For a **divorce decree or judgment**, obtain the filed record and necessary attachments from the **originating Superior Court**. CDPH's limited 1962–June 1984 Certificate of Record is a different document and does not establish that the divorce became final. [CDPH issuer page](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CHSI/Pages/Vital-Records-Obtaining-Certified-Copies-of-Divorce-Records.aspx) (official indexed text; direct retrieval failed) · [CDC directory, Divorce](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/w2w/california.htm) (document distinction only, not its fee).

| Stage | Supported branch and stopping point |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary court certification | Orange County describes a certification stamp/court seal signed and dated by a **Deputy Clerk** on the specified copy. Order the court-issued certified record; an online case summary or private photocopy is not it. |
| Exemplification/authentication package | Orange describes a separate document-listing form prepared by a deputy and signed by a **Superior Court Judge**. Stanislaus optional **G003 (Rev 02/04)** illustrates deputy copy attestation/seal → judge authentication of deputy → deputy authentication of judge/seal. Those clerks are Superior Court officials, not a separate County Clerk by assumption. |
| SOS signature check | Identify the actual certifier and the signature SOS will authenticate. When that signature and paper form are confirmed acceptable, submit the complete court-certified package. If recognition or an intermediate step is unresolved, obtain the court/SOS direction in **CA-Q2** before representing it as ready. No universal direct-deputy acceptance, mandatory exemplification, or County Clerk step is established here. |
| Electronic court record | Online ordering is not proof of an accepted self-print route. Request the court-issued paper counterpart or exact SOS presentation instructions; preserve the certification and attachments. |

[Orange copy FAQ, p.2](https://www.occourts.org/system/files?file=fl_online_copywork_faqs.pdf) · [Orange Family Law Records](https://www.occourts.org/divisions/family-law/family-law-records) · [Stanislaus G003](https://www.stanislaus.courts.ca.gov/system/files/certificate_authentication.pdf) · [current forms catalog](https://www.stanislaus.courts.ca.gov/forms-filing/common-forms-packets). G003 and its catalog were checked through official indexed text; direct PDF access returned 403. It is a concrete local example, not proof of a mandatory or sufficient statewide apostille chain. JSON branches `court-divorce-judgment` and `court-exemplification` preserve that limit.

Orange's online/mail/telephone copy orders produce mailed documents; mail requests need identification of the copies, fees and a sufficiently stamped addressed envelope. Confidential/sealed records retain their access restrictions. Its undated FAQ says **1–3 court days**, while the current records page says **30–45 days**. Neither becomes a deadline promise. Court-copy delivery and SOS return are separate stages. A recipient's demand for exemplification, finality evidence or translation is separate from SOS signature authentication. Do not transplant the birth/death county-certification exception onto court records.

## Education: issuer record versus private statement

For an institutional record, start with the issuing registrar's own certification/notarization procedure. The academic issuer establishes the record's provenance; the subsequent California apostille authenticates the **California notary's signature** in the documented notarial routes. A student's statement about an attached transcript is a different document. JSON branches: `education-issuer-notarized`, `education-private-statement`, `education-digital-or-direct`.

| Record and issuer example | Supported preparation and limit |
| --- | --- |
| UC Davis transcript or verification | Obtain a newly generated original for its Registrar notarization service. Previously issued reproductions are not accepted for that service. |
| UC Davis diploma | Supply the copy its Registrar requests for authentication and notarization. This is input to the issuer's procedure, not permission to send a bare photocopy to SOS. UC Davis offers mail or authorized pickup; its page excludes rush/in-person notarization. |
| UC Berkeley records | Its Registrar offers notarization of official transcripts, verifications and diplomas/diploma copies and explicitly identifies the California notary signature as the apostille target. |
| Caltech records | Its published procedure prepares a registrar certifying cover letter, arranges notarization, and mails to SOS; SOS returns to the requester. Use that procedure for issuer preparation, not the page's overly broad opening definition of apostille as true-copy certification. SOS FAQ 1 controls authentication's effect. |
| Notary-only academic copy certificate | Do not treat this as an eligible issuer record or silently assume a declarant affidavit. Replace it through the issuer procedure; any lawful private declaration is a different document. JSON branch `education-notary-copy-certificate`. |
| Student/custodian education affidavit | Label and route it as a private statement. It does not become the institution's certified record. Establish that the recipient actually accepts that substitute. |
| Digital transcript/diploma or unnotarized registrar signature | Direct authentication of every public registrar's signature and electronic-to-paper presentation are **not established** here. Obtain output/signer-specific instructions; a verification code or an issuer's general view that an apostille is unnecessary is not recipient acceptance. |

Sources checked October 22: [UC Davis](https://registrar.ucdavis.edu/records/notary) (page updated September 8, 2025), [UC Berkeley](https://www.registrar.berkeley.edu/academic-records/notary-services/), [Caltech Procedure](https://registrar.caltech.edu/records/apostille). These examples cover their own records, not every public/private institution. None establishes a mandatory county-clerk stage for the cited registrar-notarization routes. Do not infer that an exceptional signer or output will be accepted without checking it. Confirm any recipient-required sealed-envelope condition before opening or forwarding a package.

The [2026 SOS Notary Public Handbook](https://notary.cdn.sos.ca.gov/forms/notary-handbook-current.pdf), printed pp.17–19 and 31–32, does not grant general notary authority to certify diploma copies. Its separate Government Code **27201.1** tangible-copy procedure uses a custodian declaration/jurat for recording; it is not evidence that a printed academic record qualifies for apostille or becomes issuer-certified. Institution certification followed by notarization must remain distinct from a notary personally certifying a copy.

## Destination, unresolved questions and limits

California uses a single Apostille output for foreign destinations ([FAQ 2](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/apostille-faqs)). That output name does not resolve treaty applicability or a non-Convention legalization chain. Check current [HCCH applicability](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41) for the actual destination/date; this increment did not refresh the retained September 29 treaty-source check. Obtain separate recipient evidence for document type, age, translation, sealed delivery and acceptance. No transaction-specific recipient evidence is supplied here.

Public evidence questions are recorded in the [October 22 notes](source-notes-2026-10-22.md), [October 28 notes](source-notes-2026-10-28.md), [October 31 notes](source-notes-2026-10-31.md) and JSON `unresolvedQuestions`:

- **CA-Q1:** Exact online-certified company PDF presentation in SOS mail/counter lanes, required certification/print conditions, and agency paper alternative.
- **CA-Q2:** Exact Superior Court certifier/signature chain, any needed intermediate certification or exemplification, and accepted paper counterpart of an electronic court record.
- **CA-Q3:** Direct school-official authentication, original issuer-notarial package requirements, electronic presentation and sealed delivery.
- **CA-Q4:** Exact marriage certifier, any needed issuer certification, informational-copy and electronic presentation; separate recipient acceptance.

- **CA-Q5:** San Diego LNR indexes, years, names and confidential coverage; actual issued certifier, paper form and any necessary intermediate certification.
- **CA-Q6:** Whether CDPH offers a distinct person-based no-marriage search and the actual CNPR signature/output route.

These increments supply supported preparation branches and explicit stopping points. Marriage coverage remains limited to the documented issuer/copy routes; other issuer categories, unproven electronic outputs, destination-specific legalization and recipient decisions remain incomplete. California is still partial, and the other state pilots retain their prior dates. Source research and separate-agent review are not agency approval, a live submission test, or a legal opinion.
