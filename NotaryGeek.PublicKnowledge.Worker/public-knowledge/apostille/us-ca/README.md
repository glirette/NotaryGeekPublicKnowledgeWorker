# California apostille and authentication: agent route guide

**Reviewed September 29, 2026 · Partial California route pilot.** [Structured record](us-ca-apostille-authentication.json) · [coverage map](../coverage.json) · [Notary Geek routing model](https://notary.cx/notarial-routing-model.json)

Start with the **Public-Official Signature Route**: identify the signature on the record, its issuer, and the actual destination. The California Secretary of State authenticates California public officials' signatures for foreign use. Its apostille confirms a signature, official capacity, and seal where applicable; it does not validate a document's contents. [California request page](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/request-apostille) · [FAQ](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/apostille-faqs)

## Document branch before submission

| Presented document | Route question and action |
| --- | --- |
| California birth or death record | Is the signer a county clerk/deputy, county recorder/deputy, or State Registrar? Those signatures are listed by the Secretary of State. A health officer or local registrar signature may require the issuing county clerk to certify it, or a new certified copy from the county recorder or California Department of Public Health. Read the official title at the bottom of the record. |
| California SOS company certificate or certified filing | The [Business Entities Records Request](https://www.sos.ca.gov/business-programs/business-entities/information-requests) page offers online certified copies and certificates of status for corporations, LLCs and LPs. **Plain online copies are a different category.** For other entity types, a certified copy requires paper request and a manual certification stamp. Identify the actual official signature and confirm how the online certified output is presented for the apostille paper lane; do not assume an ordinary printout qualifies. |
| Private company operating agreement or bylaws | California says these are maintained by the entity rather than filed with the Secretary of State. If they are privately notarized, follow the notary signature, not a claimed SOS certification on the company's formation record. |
| Original notarized private document | Is the notarial act signed by a California notary on the original? Follow that notary's signature. An apostille does not prove the private document's contents. |
| Other state or federal record | Identify its issuing official and competent authority. A requester's California residence or a Sacramento courier address does not turn an out-of-state or federal signature into a California signature. This is a Notary Geek routing inference from the authority boundary. |
| Printout, scan, or electronic output | The mail instructions reject photocopies and call for an original notarized and/or certified document. Do not assume a printout of an electronic record qualifies; check the issuing authority and California Secretary of State for that exact output. |

The birth/death signer list and potential extra county step come from the [California FAQ](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/apostille-faqs). The paper original lane is described in the [request instructions](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/request-apostille).

## Mail and counter lanes

| Lane | Current agency instructions and limits |
| --- | --- |
| Mail to Sacramento | Submit the eligible document, a cover sheet naming the destination country, $20 per Apostille by check or money order, and a self-addressed return envelope. USPS goes to the published P.O. Box; FedEx/UPS/DHL go to the published 1500 11th Street address. Include prepaid postage for tracked return, or the office uses regular USPS. Mail requests are processed in receipt order; overnight carriage does not expedite agency processing. |
| Sacramento counter | Same-day in-person service at 1500 11th Street, 3rd Floor. $20 per Apostille plus $6 per **different public official's signature** authenticated; use the destination-country cover sheet. |
| Los Angeles counter | Same-day in-person service, no mail. The general address is 300 South Spring Street, Room 12513, but the office currently flags temporary first-floor **suite 1215** service due to an elevator outage. Check the current location before visiting. The same $20 and signature-based $6 charge applies; cash is not accepted there. |

The $6 special handling fee is for in-person requests only. Two documents bearing the same public official's signature can incur two $20 Apostille fees but one $6 signature fee, as the [FAQ examples](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/apostille-faqs) show. An individual may present someone else's document without being related to its subject. See the [request page](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/request-apostille) and [Los Angeles office notice](https://www.sos.ca.gov/business-programs/los-angeles-office).

The [processing page](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/processing-times), **last updated September 28, 2026**, said Sacramento was processing mail received September 14, while counter requests were *typically* processed within 30 minutes after submission. Those are dated agency observations, not an end-to-end delivery estimate or promised deadline.

## Destination and evidence boundary

California says it issues a **single Apostille for documents used outside the United States regardless of destination country**. Keep the state output separate from the receiving-country route. Check the [HCCH Convention status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41) for applicability, then ask the recipient what exact record, translation, age, and any further outside-Convention legalization it requires. California's label by itself does not settle those requirements. The office does not issue an apostille for use in Puerto Rico or another U.S. territory. [California FAQ](https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/apostille-faqs)

**Recipient Evidence** remains transaction-specific. An agency apostille is evidence about a signature, not written acceptance by the receiving school, employer, court, registry, or consulate. Do not convert an unverified remote/electronic output into a claimed **Paper Original Lane**.

## Scope and next research

This pilot covers the distinctive birth/death signature exception, California's destination-neutral state output, mail/counter fees and timing, and temporary Los Angeles location. Marriage/divorce, education, court records, exact electronic certified output acceptance, and destination-specific legalization require additional issuer-specific research. The business branch now distinguishes SOS certified copies from plain images and private company documents. Recheck all forms, fees, queue data, office notices, and recipient rules at use time.
