# Delaware apostille and authentication: agent route guide

**Reviewed September 29, 2026 UTC · Partial Delaware route pilot.** [Structured record](us-de-apostille-authentication.json) · [coverage map](../coverage.json) · [Notary Geek routing model](https://notary.cx/notarial-routing-model.json)

The Delaware Division of Corporations handles the **Public-Official Signature Route** for Delaware notaries and Delaware public officials. First identify the issuer and signature on the exact document, then distinguish a filed company record, other official copy, and a privately notarized document. The destination determines whether Delaware calls for an apostille (Convention path) or authentication (outside-Convention path). Further U.S. Department of State authentication may be needed for some non-Convention destinations. [Division guidance](https://corp.delaware.gov/apost_info/) · [HCCH status](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41)

## Document and issuer branches

| Record offered | Delaware's rule and next question |
| --- | --- |
| Filed Delaware company record | **Each filing must be certified separately.** A set of filings under one cover certification is unacceptable for apostille. Obtain the actual Division certified records, then ask for the correct apostille/authentication per document. |
| Delaware State or County public record | It must be an **official copy from the issuing public office**. Do not substitute a notarized photocopy for the official-copy route. The Division's FAQ includes certified Delaware vital records and official State background checks in the personal-use examples. |
| Private paper document notarized by a Delaware notary | Confirm the original notarial act and the recipient's requirements. The Division expressly **does not accept remotely or electronically notarized documents for apostille**. Do not infer agency acceptance from a notarial act's legality elsewhere. |
| Document in another language | The Division requires an English translation, with **both** language versions notarized; it does not supply translation. Confirm how this fits an official record and recipient request before changing the document. |
| Out-of-state, federal, foreign-issued, or photocopied record | These are listed as outside the Division's remit. Find the authority for the signature actually on the record. Delaware incorporation or a Delaware registered-agent address cannot turn an out-of-state or federal signature into a Delaware signature. This last sentence is a Notary Geek routing inference. |

Sources: [Delaware overview](https://corp.delaware.gov/apost_info/) and [non-commercial submission instructions](https://corp.delaware.gov/submitting-non-commercial-documents-for-apostille-or-authentication/). The Division also excludes documents for use *within U.S. states*, while explicitly carving out territories and possessions from that stated exclusion.

## Personal and commercial submission lanes

| Lane | Current published procedure |
| --- | --- |
| Personal, non-commercial | The dedicated page says **$30 for personal-use documents presented simultaneously**. For mail, send a cover memo with purpose and destination country to Division of Corporations, John G. Townsend Building, 401 Federal Street, Suite 4, Dover, DE 19901. It says mail is typically processed the same business day and returned first-class unless a prepaid Priority USPS, FedEx, or UPS envelope is supplied. Hand delivery requires an appointment at **302-739-3077**, and the page says personal requests are typically processed the same business day. |
| Commercial or other non-personal | The dedicated page says **$30 per document**, with an additional charge for commercial expediting. Separately identify the certified record procurement and apostille request. The [general certificate/request instructions](https://corp.delaware.gov/regguide/) require a cover memo and destination country for apostille/gold seal. They require payment and say credit cards use the online document filing/certificate request service, not mail or walk-in. |
| Notary Good Standing | Some destinations require proof of the Delaware notary's appointment. The Division says to check with the receiving territory/country before ordering this separate certificate. |

See the [non-commercial procedure](https://corp.delaware.gov/submitting-non-commercial-documents-for-apostille-or-authentication/), [general request instructions](https://corp.delaware.gov/regguide/), and [overview](https://corp.delaware.gov/apost_info/). A **Paper Original Lane** for a notarized document differs from an official certified-copy lane for a filed or publicly recorded document.

**Timing conflict:** The [Division FAQ](https://corp.delaware.gov/faqs/) says hand-delivered personal/adoption requests are completed within two hours; the dedicated [non-commercial procedure](https://corp.delaware.gov/submitting-non-commercial-documents-for-apostille-or-authentication/) says appointment required and *typically* same business day. The dedicated procedure is a safer planning baseline, and a time-critical request needs direct confirmation. Neither statement covers transit or recipient acceptance.

## Destination and recipient evidence

Use the current [HCCH status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41) and the destination's actual requirements. Delaware describes authentication for non-Convention countries and says some may need a later U.S. Department of State step. A notary Good Standing certificate, translation, record age, or consular step must come from **Recipient Evidence** for the transaction; do not add or omit one by habit. An apostille authenticates a signature route, not a promise that a registry or consulate will accept the underlying document.

## Scope and further work

This pilot resolves the separately certified filing rule, public-copy versus notarized-private-document route, remote/electronic apostille exclusion, language pair, and personal/commercial fee split. It does not establish acceptance of any particular electronic **official certified copy**, a recipient's document standards, a corporate certificate's procurement fee, or a guaranteed turnaround. Recheck each volatile procedure and resolve the FAQ timing discrepancy before action. This is public guidance, not an order or service commitment.
