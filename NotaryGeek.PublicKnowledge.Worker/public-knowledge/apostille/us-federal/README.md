# U.S. federal apostille and authentication: identify the federal issuer

**Reviewed:** September 28, 2026 (U.S. Eastern). This is a public research guide and [structured source record](us-federal-apostille-authentication.json). It does not accept a request or promise an appointment, processing date, or foreign recipient result.

## Federal is more than one office

The [HCCH United States competent-authority record](https://www.hcch.net/en/states/authorities/details3/?aid=353) names three distinct federal-origin routes:

| Signature or record | Designated apostille authority | First research step |
|---|---|---|
| Document issued by a U.S. federal agency | U.S. Department of State **Office of Authentications** | Check [federal document preparation](https://travel.state.gov/en/replace-certify/authenticate-documents/apostille-certificate.html) and the [current request procedure](https://travel.state.gov/en/replace-certify/authenticate-documents/request-services.html). |
| Record issued by a U.S. federal court | Clerk or deputy clerk of the federal court | Identify the issuing court and check its current apostille/certification process. The State Department also lists federal court documents in its examples, so do not assert one universal court route without the issuing court’s instructions. |
| Consular Report of Birth, Death, or Marriage | U.S. Department of State **Passport Services, Vital Records Section** | Begin with the [consular life-event records](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/replace-certify-docs/requesting-a-record.html) office. Do not assume the generic DS-4194 mail route handles this distinct record. |

A **state-issued** birth record, company record, or state notarial act stays on its state or territorial competent-authority route for the Convention path. Washington, D.C. is a place, not a shortcut that makes a document federal. For a destination outside the Convention path, [State Department authentication guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/replace-certify-docs/authenticate-your-document/authentication-certificate-requirements.html) discusses a state authentication before its federal authentication step; that is separate research, not an “apostille plus more” rule.

## Prepare the actual federal record

The [State Department preparation page](https://travel.state.gov/en/replace-certify/authenticate-documents/apostille-certificate.html) lists federal examples including FBI background checks, USDA certificates, IRS tax-residency certification, and naturalization documents. It calls for an **original or certified copy** with legible official signature, printed name/title, agency seal, issue date, and original seals/signatures. It warns against notarizing the original federal document. A notarized cover sheet authenticates a different signature; it does not convert an ordinary scan or altered federal original into an official federal record.

For an FBI result, ask whether the output presented is the federal agency’s qualifying signed/sealed record. For a federal court record, ask which clerk issued it. For a consular report, ask which Passport Services vital record is needed. Those are different **Public-Official Signature Routes** even though all may be described loosely as “federal apostilles.”

## Current Office of Authentications request details

The [State Department request page, updated September 1, 2026](https://travel.state.gov/en/replace-certify/authenticate-documents/request-services.html), asks for **Form DS-4194** with destination country, the documents, and **$20 per document**. Its mail return instructions call for a prepaid **USPS or UPS** envelope and specifically exclude FedEx for that return. It describes processing by mail within **five weeks after office receipt**, routine walk-in drop-off/pick-up within **seven business days**, and same-day appointments only for a narrow immediate-family life-or-death travel emergency. Check the current page before using any fee, address, carrier, or timing detail; delivery and recipient review are outside those estimates.

A proper apostille in an applicable Convention destination ends the legalization chain. For a destination outside that path, research the separate authentication and any destination-specific legalization requirements. An apostille verifies the relevant public-official signature/seal, not the contents or the recipient’s document requirements. Keep **Recipient Evidence** separate.

## Still to verify for a real case

- Current HCCH Convention applicability between the United States and the destination.
- The issuing court’s own procedure, or Passport Services Vital Records process, for their special branches.
- Whether a particular electronic agency output or printout is accepted as the required original/certified record.
- Recipient instructions on age, translation, exact record type, and output form.
