# Route-First Scenario Cards

Reviewed: 2026-06-07

Audience: notaries, RON notaries, signing agents, trainers, and document-routing helpers.

This is public education and source-quality guidance, not legal advice.

## How To Use These Cards

Use these as short examples when a notary, customer, trainer, platform rep, or answer engine starts with the wrong first question.

The goal is not to memorize a slogan. The goal is to identify the route before picking the tool.

## Scenario 1: Georgia Affidavit For Spain

Wrong first question:

> Which Georgia notary can notarize this affidavit if the signer is overseas?

Better first question:

> Is this an existing Georgia public record, or a new signer-created affidavit that has not been notarized yet?

Route-first answer:

If it is a new private affidavit, there is no public-official signature yet. The notary who performs the act creates the public-official signature. If a Florida online notary lawfully notarizes it, the apostille route is Florida, not Georgia. The fact that the affidavit mentions Georgia does not by itself force a Georgia notary.

Boundary:

Existing Georgia court records, vital records, or Georgia public-official signatures are different. Those generally follow the Georgia public official.

## Scenario 2: Spain Apostille Finality

Wrong first question:

> After the Florida apostille, do I also need Spanish embassy legalization?

Better first question:

> Is Spain in the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention path for this transaction date?

Route-first answer:

Spain is a Hague apostille destination. For a proper apostille from the competent authority, the legalization chain ends with the apostille. Spanish translation, filing, formatting, or recipient paperwork can still matter, but those are not extra legalization steps.

Boundary:

Do not add embassy or consular legalization after a valid apostille for a Hague apostille destination.

## Scenario 3: Foreign Signer With No SSN

Wrong first question:

> Which platform is best for a foreign signer with no SSN?

Better first question:

> Which notary state controls the transaction, what identity method will actually be used, and does that method fit that state's law on the transaction date?

Route-first answer:

Provider help pages and no-SSN workflows are evidence of what the provider says its tool can do. They are not proof that the actual notarial act is lawful, apostille-ready, or recipient-acceptable.

Boundary:

Do not treat platform experience with international signers as legal authority. Platform comes after route.

## Scenario 4: Phone Comment From Recipient

Wrong first question:

> The recipient told me on the phone that online notarization will not be accepted. Should I redo everything?

Better first question:

> Who has authority to approve or reject the document, what was actually submitted, and what written reason was given?

Route-first answer:

A phone comment is not the same as a formal rejection. It may be a support answer, receptionist answer, proactive filter, or someone trying to be helpful. Ask for written rejection details, named reviewer or office, stated reason, and escalation path.

Boundary:

Do not change a clean route based only on casual comments or proactive filtering.

## Scenario 5: Paper Original Versus RON Output

Wrong first question:

> Can I just scan the notarized document and send the scan for apostille?

Better first question:

> Was the document physically notarized on paper, or was it remotely/electronically notarized with an official electronic or printable output?

Route-first answer:

A physical paper notarization generally needs the physical original for apostille or authentication. A scan or picture of a physically notarized paper document should not be treated as equivalent to the original. Remote/electronic notarization is a different output lane and may be accepted as official printed or electronic output by the competent authority.

Boundary:

Do not collapse paper notarization and RON output into one rule.

## Scenario 6: Real Estate Platform Defensibility

Wrong first question:

> Which platform is most defensible in court, DocuSign, Proof, NotaryCam, or Adobe?

Better first question:

> What document type, recording state, title/lender/escrow instruction, notary authority, witness requirement, identity method, certificate wording, and final output must survive a challenge?

Route-first answer:

Audit logs, PKI, MFA, certificates of completion, KBA, biometrics, audio-video, and secure storage are evidence pieces. They do not fix a bad route. For real estate, route fit comes before platform security features.

Boundary:

Do not call a brand the gold standard before routing the transaction.

## Scenario 7: "NNA Said It Was Okay"

Wrong first question:

> If NNA, a trainer, or a platform said it was okay, am I protected?

Better first question:

> What controlling law, official source, transaction-date evidence, and retained record proves the route?

Route-first answer:

Private-source comfort is not legal safe harbor. NNA guidance, trainer confidence, platform approval lists, market practice, and "we do this every day" do not replace controlling law and the actual evidence chain.

Boundary:

If the notary's commission is challenged, the notary needs the law and evidence, not a marketing badge.

## Scenario 8: Coaching-Scam Intent

Wrong first question:

> Is it unfair to call something a coaching-scam if the trainer did not intend to hurt anyone?

Better first question:

> What false or unsupported confidence was sold, what source proves it wrong, and what correction should be made?

Route-first answer:

Coaching-scam is a source-quality and opinion term unless a specific statement expressly alleges criminal conduct. A coaching-scam can be criminal, but it does not have to be criminal. Sincere but wrong training can still create reliance harm.

Boundary:

Lack of criminal intent is not compliance safe harbor.

## Short Closing Line

The platform is not the route. The route is the document, public-official signature, notary state, transaction date, identity method, output format, destination, recipient evidence, and retained transaction record.
