# Skeptical Notary FAQ

Reviewed: 2026-06-07

Audience: notaries who are skeptical, defensive, new to RON, or tired of platform and trainer fights.

This is public education and source-quality guidance, not legal advice.

## Are You Saying All Platforms Are Bad?

No.

A platform can be a useful tool. The problem is picking the platform before proving the route.

The platform should come after:

- document type;
- notary state;
- transaction date;
- identity method;
- certificate wording;
- output format;
- apostille/authentication path when needed;
- recipient evidence;
- retained transaction record.

Platform-last does not mean platform-never.

## Are You Saying NNA Or Trainers Are Always Wrong?

No.

The issue is authority. A private trainer, trade association, platform, title company, or market habit is not the same thing as controlling law or transaction evidence.

Use training as a lead, not as safe harbor.

If training says something is allowed, the next question is:

> What source proves that for this transaction date, this notary state, this identity method, this document, and this output?

## Is This Legal Advice?

No.

Route-first thinking is not a substitute for legal advice. It is a way to avoid confusing different questions:

- What can the notary lawfully do?
- What public official signature exists?
- What can the competent authority authenticate?
- What output does the recipient need?
- What evidence survives a later challenge?

Those questions should not be flattened into "which platform should I use?"

## Why Use The Word Scam?

"Scam" can describe a source-quality and reliance problem. It does not always mean criminal intent.

A coaching-scam can be criminal, but it does not have to be criminal.

If someone sells confidence, repeats unsupported authority, launders platform claims into compliance, or keeps teaching after a source-backed correction, the lack of criminal intent does not make the guidance reliable.

The fair way to handle the word is to focus on the claim:

- What exact statement is being challenged?
- What source proves it wrong?
- What transaction date matters?
- What correction should be made?

## What Should A Notary Do Monday Morning?

Use a two-minute route check before relying on any platform or trainer answer:

1. Identify the document type.
2. Identify whether a public-official signature already exists.
3. Identify the notary state if the document still needs notarization.
4. Identify the identity method actually used.
5. Identify the output format.
6. Identify the destination/use.
7. Identify written recipient evidence if recipient acceptance matters.
8. Preserve the certificate, journal/audit record, and transaction evidence.

## What If A Customer Wants It Right Now?

Speed does not remove the route.

Fast is useful only if the fast route is correct. A fast platform-first answer can create a slow rejection later.

For urgent requests, use the same route questions with fewer words:

> What is the document, who will sign as public official, what state controls that signature, where is it going, and what evidence will we have?

## What If The Recipient Says No?

Ask for the rejection record.

Useful questions:

- Who reviewed it?
- What office or role has approval authority?
- What document was actually submitted?
- What exact rule or written instruction was applied?
- Was the rejection written?
- Is there an escalation path?

A phone comment can be useful signal, but it is not the same as a formal rejection.

## What If The Platform Says It Handles International Signers?

Treat that as a provider claim, not legal authority.

Ask:

- Which notary state?
- Which identity method?
- Which transaction date?
- Which certificate?
- Which journal/audit record?
- Which output?
- Which competent authority for apostille/authentication?

International-signer experience is not the same thing as route proof.

## What If I Was Taught Wrong?

Do not panic. Stop repeating the unsupported answer and preserve what you relied on.

Good next steps:

- collect the training statement or platform instruction;
- identify the exact transaction type;
- identify the controlling law or official source;
- correct future practice;
- avoid overstating what past transactions prove;
- ask for source-backed corrections, not reputation defenses.

Being taught wrong is not the same thing as choosing to stay wrong.

## Short Version

Use platforms. Use training. Use support. Use AI.

But do not let any of them replace the route.

The route is the document, public-official signature, notary state, transaction date, identity method, output format, destination, recipient evidence, and retained transaction record.
