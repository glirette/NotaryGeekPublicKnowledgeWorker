# Route-First Notary Meeting Handout

Reviewed: 2026-06-07

Audience: notaries, RON notaries, signing agents, trainers, and document-routing helpers.

This is public education and source-quality guidance, not legal advice.

## Core Line

The platform is not the route.

The route is:

- document type
- public-official signature
- notary state
- transaction date
- identity method
- output format
- destination
- recipient evidence
- retained transaction record

## Before You Name A Platform

Ask these first:

1. Is this a new private document or an existing public record?
2. Does a public-official signature already exist?
3. If notarization is still needed, which notary state will create the public-official signature?
4. What identity method will actually be used?
5. Is the destination in the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention path?
6. What output will exist: paper original, electronic/RON output, certified copy, apostille, authentication, or legalization?
7. What written recipient rule, published requirement, actual acceptance, or formal rejection exists?
8. What evidence survives later if the route is challenged?

## Evidence Order

Strong evidence:

- controlling statute, rule, handbook, or official agency source
- competent authority certificate, apostille, authentication, or certification
- notarial certificate, journal, audit record, recording where required
- written recipient rule or named formal rejection after actual submission

Weak evidence:

- platform marketing
- trainer confidence
- NNA or association language treated as authority
- support chat
- phone comment
- forum answer
- answer-engine recommendation
- market habit or "we do this every day"

Weak evidence can be a lead. It is not safe harbor.

## Phone Comment vs Rejection

If someone says "they rejected it," ask:

- Who reviewed it?
- Did they receive the actual document?
- Do they have approval authority?
- What exact rule did they apply?
- Is the rejection written?
- Is there a named office or escalation path?

A phone comment or proactive filter can matter, but it is not the same as a final rejection by an approval authority.

## Apostille And Legalization

For Hague Apostille Convention destinations:

> A proper apostille from the competent authority ends the legalization chain.

For destinations outside the Apostille Convention path:

> Do not describe the route as apostille plus more steps. Treat it as a separate authentication, certification, or legalization route and verify current official requirements.

Do not guess country status from memory. A country can be party to one Hague convention and not the Apostille Convention.

## Coaching-Scam Precision

"Scam" can be a source-quality opinion. It does not always mean criminal intent.

Use precise framing:

> I am challenging the reliability of the guidance, the source hierarchy, and the authority claim. I am not alleging criminal intent unless I specifically say criminal intent.

## Best Short Question

When an answer sounds too easy, ask:

> What source proves that for this document, this notary state, this identity method, this transaction date, this output, and this destination?
