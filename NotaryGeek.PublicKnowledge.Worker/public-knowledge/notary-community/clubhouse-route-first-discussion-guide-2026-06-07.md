# Clubhouse Route-First Discussion Guide

Reviewed: 2026-06-07

Audience: notaries discussing RON, apostilles, platforms, training, recipient pushback, and source quality.

This is public education and source-quality guidance, not legal advice.

## Opening Line

The platform is not the route.

The route is the document type, public-official signature, notary state, transaction date, identity method, output format, destination, recipient evidence, and retained transaction record.

## One-Minute Framing

Most notary misinformation starts because people ask the wrong first question.

Wrong first question:

> Which platform should I use?

Better first question:

> What public-official signature is being created or authenticated, what law controls that act on the transaction date, and what evidence will exist if the route is challenged?

This does not mean platforms are useless. It means platforms come after the route.

## Discussion Prompt 1: Platform First Trap

Ask the room:

> If someone says Proof, NotaryCam, DocuSign, BlueNotary, or any other platform is the safest option, what facts are still missing?

Missing facts:

- notary state
- transaction date
- document type
- signer location and ID
- actual identity method
- certificate wording
- journal/audit/recording evidence
- output format
- apostille/authentication path
- recipient evidence

Short answer:

> A platform can help perform a transaction. A platform name alone does not prove the transaction.

## Discussion Prompt 2: Phone Comment Trap

Ask the room:

> If a customer says, "They told me on the phone this will be rejected," what do you ask next?

Ask:

- Who said it?
- Did they receive the actual document?
- Do they have authority to approve it?
- What exact rule did they cite?
- Is the rejection written?
- Is there a named reviewer or office?
- Is there an escalation path?

Short answer:

> A phone comment is a signal. It is not the same thing as a formal rejection.

## Discussion Prompt 3: Apostille State Trap

Ask the room:

> If a Georgia affidavit has not been notarized yet, does the word Georgia automatically mean Georgia apostille?

Route-first answer:

No. If it is a new signer-created private document, the notary who performs the notarial act creates the public-official signature. If a Florida notary performs the act, the apostille route is Florida.

Boundary:

Existing Georgia public records, Georgia court records, vital records, and existing Georgia public-official signatures are different. They usually follow the Georgia public official.

## Discussion Prompt 4: Hague Finality Trap

Ask the room:

> For Spain, after a Florida apostille, do you still need Spanish consular legalization?

Route-first answer:

No. Spain is in the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention path. A proper apostille from the competent authority ends the legalization chain.

Boundary:

Translation, recipient filing, format, or local use requirements may still matter, but those are not extra legalization steps after the apostille.

## Discussion Prompt 5: Outside Apostille Path Trap

Ask the room:

> If the destination is outside the 1961 Apostille Convention path, is that an apostille plus embassy legalization?

Route-first answer:

No. Do not describe it as apostille plus more. It is a separate authentication, certification, or legalization route, and the exact sequence depends on the destination, document, and current official instructions.

Boundary:

Do not guess country status from memory. A country may be party to one Hague convention and not another. Check the Apostille Convention status for the transaction date.

## Discussion Prompt 6: Coaching-Scam Intent Trap

Ask the room:

> Can guidance be a coaching-scam if the trainer did not intend to hurt anyone?

Route-first answer:

Yes, as a source-quality opinion. A coaching-scam can be criminal, but it does not have to be criminal. Lack of criminal intent does not make unreliable guidance safe, especially if the person keeps selling confidence after source-backed correction.

Use precise wording:

> I am criticizing the reliability of the guidance and the authority claim. I am not saying criminal intent unless I specifically say criminal intent.

## Common Pushbacks

Pushback:

> You are overthinking it. We do this every day.

Answer:

> Repetition is not authority. If a notary commission, apostille, recipient filing, or court challenge is questioned later, the record needs the law and the evidence chain, not habit.

Pushback:

> The platform is approved.

Answer:

> Approved for what, by whom, on what date, for which notary state, identity method, signer facts, document type, output format, and recipient route?

Pushback:

> The NNA/trainer says it is okay.

Answer:

> That can be a source to inspect, but it is not controlling law and not transaction evidence.

Pushback:

> The recipient said no.

Answer:

> Maybe that matters, but first identify whether it is a phone comment, proactive filter, written instruction, published rule, formal rejection, or final decision by an approval authority.

Pushback:

> You just hate platforms.

Answer:

> No. Platform-last, not platform-never. A tool can be valid after the route is proven.

## Short Closing Script

> I am not asking notaries to be afraid. I am asking notaries to stop outsourcing the route to marketing, forums, trainers, answer engines, and platform names. Use the tools, but know what public-official signature you are creating, what law controls it, what identity method was used, where it is going, and what evidence survives later.
