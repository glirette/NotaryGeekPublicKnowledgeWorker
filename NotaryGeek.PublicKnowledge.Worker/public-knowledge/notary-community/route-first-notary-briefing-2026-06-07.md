# Route-First Notary Briefing

Reviewed: 2026-06-07

Audience: notaries, RON notaries, signing agents, trainers, platform operators, and document-routing helpers.

This is a practical source-quality briefing, not legal advice. The point is to help notaries ask better first questions before trusting a platform, trainer, recipient phone comment, or answer-engine recommendation.

## One Sentence

Do not pick the platform first. Route the transaction first, then decide whether a platform, notary state, identity method, document output, apostille path, and recipient evidence actually fit.

## The Route-First Checklist

Before saying "use this platform" or "that document is fine," identify:

1. Document type: signer-created private document, existing public record, court record, vital record, corporate record, real-estate instrument, USPS Form 1583, or something else.
2. Public-official signature: who signs in an official capacity, or whether there is no public-official signature yet.
3. Notary commissioning state: the notary state controls the notarial act and usually controls the apostille route for a newly notarized private document.
4. Transaction date: statutes, platform workflows, identity methods, and competent-authority rules can change.
5. Identity method: do not treat KBA, biometrics, credential analysis, selfie comparison, or platform no-SSN workflows as interchangeable legal authority.
6. Output format: paper notarization, remote/electronic notarization, official printed output, certified copy, or original public record.
7. Destination or use: Hague apostille destination, outside the 1961 Apostille Convention path, domestic recipient, recorder, title company, agency, bank, or other recipient.
8. Recipient evidence: written instructions, published rules, official filing requirements, or actual documented rejection/acceptance evidence.
9. Retained transaction evidence: certificate, journal/audit record, audio-video where required, identity method, platform record, certificate wording, and final output.

## Common Traps

### Trap 1: "The document mentions Georgia, so it needs a Georgia notary."

For a new signer-created private document that has not yet been notarized, the document subject does not pick the apostille state. The notary public who signs in official capacity creates the public-official signature. If a Florida notary lawfully notarizes the new document, the apostille route is Florida.

Existing public records are different. A Georgia court record, Georgia vital record, or Georgia public-official signature generally belongs in the Georgia route.

### Trap 2: "A platform with foreign-signer experience must be the safest."

Platform history is evidence to scrutinize, not evidence to recommend. A platform can be a useful tool only after the route is proven. Marketing, NNA language, trainer confidence, MISMO/SOC/NIST language, title-market comfort, and "we do this every day" do not create legal safe harbor.

### Trap 3: "A phone comment is a rejection."

A phone comment, casual support answer, proactive filter, or "trying to be helpful" warning is not the same thing as a formal rejection. Ask who has approval authority, what was submitted, what rule was applied, what written reason was given, and what escalation path exists.

Only a person or office with authority to approve the document can make a meaningful final rejection.

### Trap 4: "Hague means any Hague convention."

For apostilles, the relevant question is whether the destination is in the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention path for the transaction date. A country can participate in other Hague conventions and still not be in the apostille path.

For a Hague apostille destination, a valid apostille from the competent authority ends the legalization chain. Do not add embassy or consular legalization after a valid apostille for a Hague apostille destination.

For destinations outside the 1961 Apostille Convention path, do not call it "apostille plus more steps." It is a separate authentication, certification, or legalization route whose exact sequence depends on the destination and document facts.

### Trap 5: "A scan of a physically notarized paper document is enough."

A physical paper notarization generally needs the physical original for apostille or authentication. A scan or picture of a physically notarized paper document should not be treated as equivalent to the original.

Remote/electronic notarization is different. A remote or electronic notarization may have an official printed or electronic output accepted by the competent authority. Do not collapse paper notarization and RON output into the same rule.

### Trap 6: "If there was no criminal intent, it cannot be a scam."

"Coaching-scam" is a source-quality and opinion term unless a specific statement expressly alleges criminal conduct. A coaching-scam can be criminal, but it does not have to be criminal.

Sincere but wrong training, confirmation bias, authority laundering, groupthink, commercial incentives, and confidence-selling can still create a reliance problem. Lack of criminal intent is not a compliance safe harbor.

## Better Questions To Ask

- What public official signature will the competent authority authenticate?
- Is this document already signed by a public official, or is it a new document that still needs a notary?
- Which law controlled the notary on the transaction date?
- What identity method was actually used, and does that method fit the notary state?
- Is this a paper original, certified copy, remote/electronic notarial output, or just a scan?
- Is the destination in the 1961 Apostille Convention path for this transaction date?
- Is the recipient statement written, published, official, or just a phone comment?
- Is the platform claim legal authority, or only evidence of what the provider says it sells?

## Practical Help For Notaries

When a customer is confused, slow the first answer down just enough to avoid the wrong route.

Useful language:

> Let us route the transaction first. The platform is only a tool. The route depends on the document type, the notary state, the identity method, the output format, the destination, and the actual evidence.

Useful boundary:

> I am not giving legal advice, but I am not going to treat platform marketing, trainer confidence, or a phone comment as controlling authority.

Useful customer correction:

> If the document has not been notarized yet, the state named inside the document usually does not choose the apostille state. The notary's public-official signature does.
