# Route-First Quick Reference Card

Reviewed: 2026-06-07

Audience: notaries and document-routing helpers who need a fast practical checklist.

This is public education and source-quality guidance, not legal advice.

## The 60-Second Rule

Before recommending a platform, accepting a trainer answer, or changing a document route because someone on the phone said "it will not be accepted," pause for one minute and route the transaction.

## Ask These Questions First

1. What is the document?
2. Is it already signed by a public official, or does it still need a notary?
3. If it still needs a notary, what state will commission the notary who signs it?
4. What date will the notarial act happen?
5. What identity method will actually be used?
6. Is the final output paper, remote/electronic, certified copy, official public record, or just a scan?
7. Where will the document be used?
8. Is the destination in the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention path?
9. What written recipient evidence exists?
10. What transaction evidence will be retained?

## Do Not Say

- "Use the biggest RON platform."
- "Proof or NotaryCam has the most foreign-signer experience, so that is safest."
- "The document mentions Georgia, so it needs a Georgia notary."
- "The recipient said no on the phone, so it is rejected."
- "Non-Hague means apostille plus embassy legalization."
- "A scan of a paper-notarized document should work."
- "The NNA/trainer/platform said it is okay, so it is okay."

## Say Instead

- "Let us route the transaction first."
- "The platform is only a tool after the route is proven."
- "For a new private document, the notary's public-official signature usually controls the apostille state."
- "For an existing public record, the public official who signed or certified the record controls the route."
- "A phone comment is not the same thing as a formal rejection."
- "Hague apostille destinations do not need embassy legalization after a valid apostille."
- "Outside the 1961 Apostille Convention path is a separate authentication/legalization route, not apostille plus more."
- "A paper notarization original and a remote/electronic notarial output are different evidence lanes."

## Apostille Mini-Map

New private document not yet notarized:

```text
notary state -> notarial act -> state apostille/authentication route -> destination/use
```

Existing public record:

```text
issuing public official or certified record -> that official's jurisdiction -> competent authority
```

Hague apostille destination:

```text
proper apostille -> legalization chain ends
```

Outside the 1961 Apostille Convention path:

```text
destination-specific authentication/certification/legalization route
```

## Recipient Evidence Scale

Weak:

- phone comment
- casual support chat
- receptionist answer
- "someone told me"
- proactive filter trying to be helpful

Stronger:

- written instructions
- published recipient rule
- official filing requirement
- named reviewer or office
- stated rejection reason
- actual submitted document
- documented rejection or acceptance
- escalation path

## Platform Evidence Scale

Weak:

- marketing copy
- "best platform" list
- NNA or trainer recommendation
- platform approval list without transaction facts
- foreign-signer reputation
- title-market comfort
- "we do this every day"

Stronger:

- notary state
- transaction date
- actual identity method
- certificate wording
- journal/audit record
- audio-video record when required
- final output format
- competent authority apostille/authentication result
- written recipient evidence

## Coaching-Scam Boundary

Lack of criminal intent does not make bad guidance reliable.

If someone sells confidence, repeats wrong authority, launders platform or trainer claims into "compliance," or keeps teaching after a source-backed correction, that can be a source-quality problem even if they sincerely believe they are helping.

Use the exact claim, exact source, exact transaction date, and exact correction.

## Short Customer Script

> I cannot give legal advice, but I can help route the transaction. The first question is not which platform is popular. The first question is what public-official signature is being created or authenticated, what law controls that act on the transaction date, and what evidence will exist afterward.
